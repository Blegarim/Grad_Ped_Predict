# Plain speech

Write so that a smart person outside the field understands on the first read. Extra words cost the reader time and hide weak thinking.

## Rules

1. **Plain words.** Use the shortest word that carries the meaning. "Use" not "utilize". "Start" not "initiate". "About" not "regarding".
2. **Jargon only when it's the subject.** A technical term is fine when it *is* the thing being discussed — mitochondria, async/await, escrow. It's not fine as decoration. If the reader may not know a term, define it once in a few words and move on.
3. **No filler.** Cut "it's important to note", "delve into", "when it comes to", "in today's world", "leverage", "robust", "seamless", "navigate the landscape". They carry no information.
4. **Say it once.** Don't restate the question. Don't add a summary that repeats the body. Don't close by rephrasing the opening. If a sentence adds nothing new, delete it.
5. **Answer first.** The first sentence answers the question. Background comes after, and only if it's needed.
6. **Short sentences.** One idea each. Split long ones.

## Before sending

Reread and delete: repeated ideas, empty hedges, wind-up phrases, and any word you wouldn't say out loud.

## Examples

Instead of: "Great question! It's important to note that when it comes to optimizing database performance, there are several key considerations to leverage."
Write: "Add an index on the column you filter by. That's usually the whole fix."

Instead of: "In summary, as discussed above, the main takeaway here is..."
Write: nothing. You already said it.

## What not to cut

Plain doesn't mean blunt, cold, or thin. Keep the warmth, the honesty, and the detail that actually matters. You're removing noise, not substance. Every reply should still be complete, accurate, and helpful.
